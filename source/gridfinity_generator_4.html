    The pack

    Measure one sheet flat and the stack you want to hold. Every number here can be typed as well as dragged, so a caliper reading goes in as it reads. Sizing from these picks the smallest footprint in half cell steps and the shortest height in units that still swallows the stack.

    Either give one sheet's thickness and how many you want, or measure the whole stack once and give that height. Switching carries the number across, so you can set it one way and check it the other.

    A folded foil sachet is thicker than it looks. Stack ten, measure, divide.
    The whole pack measured on the bench, squashed no harder than it will sit in the box.

    Front scoop

    Scoop the front wall
    The top edge dips on a raised cosine that leaves the rim with no corner, so a thumb can push the stack up and the last few sheets are still reachable.
    0 gives a V that bottoms out at a point. A flat gives a trough wide enough to get a thumb into.
    How much front wall the scoop is not allowed to eat, so the bottom sheets cannot slide out.

    Box

    A fillet all the way round where the floor meets the walls. Sweeps the corner so a thin sheet does not wedge into it. Set to 0 for a square floor.

    How hard the lid clicks

    Sets how far the bead has to squeeze past the lip on the way in. Print one pair, try it, then move a step either way. This is the only setting a printer's dimensional error really moves.

    Bin

    A fillet along the front wall that sweeps the floor up so small parts come out instead of hiding in the corner. Set to 0 for a square floor.
    Stacking lip
    The rim at the top. Another bin nests into it, and it is what the lid grips.

    Slots

    A rounded bottom is a half round trough sitting on the floor, so a pen or any other round barrel beds into it instead of rattling in a square channel.

    Measure the barrel at its widest and add about 0.6 mm. The chips do that for you.
    The labels sit on this one, so give it room.
